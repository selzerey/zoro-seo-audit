# E-E-A-T review checklist (human judgement, no scores)

Use with `content-quality.md`. This is a reading checklist for one page; the answer to each question must be backed
by something you can point at on the page. The audit scripts do not score E-E-A-T and neither should you.

## Verdict words
Answer every question with one of: **yes / partly / no / unclear**.
- "partly" is the normal answer for most pages; it is not a failure.
- If you cannot quote or point to the part of the page that supports the answer, the verdict is **unclear**, not yes.
- Do not turn verdicts into a number or a percentage.

## Experience: was this made by someone who did or used the thing?
1. Does the page show first-hand material: own measurements, own data, screenshots of the real process, a worked
   example with real inputs?
2. Does it mention specifics only a practitioner would know (a step that commonly fails, a document that is asked
   for in practice, a real-world limit)?
3. Would the page lose something essential if the first-hand parts were removed?

## Helpfulness: does it solve the reader's task?
1. Is the main question answered in the first screen, without a preamble?
2. Can the reader act after reading (numbers, steps, the form or tool they need, where to apply)?
3. Is anything that the top results agree on missing (see `competitor-serp.md`)?

## Trust (YMYL pages: money, health, law, official procedures)
- Every rate, fee, limit and date links to a primary source, with the date it is valid from.
- The page says when it was last materially updated, and that date is true.
- Who publishes the site is clear (about, contact, legal pages reachable from every page).
- Calculators: the formula in the text matches the engine; a worked example proves it.

## Never
- Invent an author, expert, reviewer, credential or "medically reviewed by" line. The organisation can be the author.
- Add fake reviews, ratings or testimonials. `author-fake-risk` lists Person authors so a human confirms each one.
- Claim expertise the page does not show.

## Output
For each page: the verdicts, one line of evidence per verdict, and at most three concrete fixes ordered by effect.
